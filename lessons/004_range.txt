QUESTION: Why did I draw these two lines exactly here? What makes each one a valid level?
MY VIEW: Price returns to the old bearish order-block level, briefly trades above it, and then experiences strong bearish rejection. The rightmost candles show renewed downward pressure, with price approaching the lower support line. I would favour selling after a failed bullish retest, but avoid opening a fresh short directly into support.
Answer:
The old OB is the last opposing candle before the strong move away from that area.
The exact line is not chosen randomly.
It comes from:
- The candle body where institutional orders were likely placed.
- The origin of the displacement.
Because the zone represents the reaction area, not simply the absolute extreme.
The lowest wick may represent:
- Liquidity sweep.
- Stop hunt.
- Temporary rejection.
The support zone should be based on:
- Repeated reactions.
- Order flow origin.
- Accepted price area.
The extreme wick can be considered liquidity, but not always the true support.

- Body area = primary OB zone.
- Full candle range = extended zone.
