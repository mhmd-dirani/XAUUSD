TYPE: CONCEPT
QUESTION: Why are these marked as ob, bbr and spring? Explain each one and the dotted x level.
MY VIEW: This is a bullish reversal setup involving a bearish order block, a breaker area and a spring below support.

Answer:
Yes.
The marked areas represent:
- OB = Order Block zone
- BBR = Breaker Block Retest zone
A breaker forms when:
1. Original OB fails.
2. Price breaks through it.
3. The old OB becomes support/resistance on retest.
Use both:
- Body = preferred entry zone.
- Full candle wick = invalidation boundary.
For a conservative system:
Entry:
- body or 50% of zone.
Stop:
- beyond wick.
Correction:
Below support are usually sell-side liquidity (the stop losses of long traders).
The spring setup works because:
1. Price sweeps sell-side liquidity below support.
2. Smart money absorbs selling.
3. Price reclaims the level.
4. Bullish reversal begins.
So the correct wording:
"The spring takes sell-side liquidity below support before reversing upward."
Answer:
For a true OB failure:
- Close beyond the wick = definitive failure.
- Close beyond body = warning that the OB is weakening.
Astra should not mark an OB invalid from one wick.
Answer:
Reclaiming X alone is not enough.
A proper spring setup requires:
1. Liquidity sweep below support.
2. Price reclaims the broken level.
3. BBR/breaker area is reclaimed or respected.
4. Bullish MSS/CHOCH confirms.
Hierarchy:
- Reclaim only → potential reversal.
- Reclaim + breaker reclaim → stronger.
- Reclaim + breaker + MSS → high-quality setup.
